Privacy policy

Last updated: 20 November 2026

Short version. Ordinary users do not create a LiveCaptionN account. A separate demo sign-in is available to app reviewers. Captions and translations can be processed on your device. Remote speech and translation modes, when used, send audio or text to the configured service. Feedback you send can be public. The app uses Google AdMob for ads and Firebase for analytics, crash reporting, and performance monitoring in release builds. The app is free to install. Ad-Free and Pro are optional upgrades. Google Play handles payments for the Play edition; Stripe handles payments for the separate GitHub edition.

1. Who this policy is from

LiveCaptionN is a free, open-source Android application provided by Hartmann Studios (chartmann1590). The full source code is available on GitHub and every release is built reproducibly by GitHub Actions from a public commit you can audit.

2. What data LiveCaptionN processes

Microphone / system audio

During an active captioning session, LiveCaptionN processes audio from the microphone or, with Android’s permission, from another app’s playback. Vosk recognizes audio on your device. When a remote speech engine is active, audio is sent to that engine for recognition. Some editions can use Android’s speech recognizer, whose processing can depend on the speech service available on your device.

Transcripts

Saved captions and translations are stored in the app’s private storage and can be deleted from History or by clearing local app data. If you export your history, you control that copy separately. Android backup and device transfer depend on your system settings and the rules of the installed version; clearing local data does not delete exported copies or prior backups.

Settings

Your configuration (such as languages, overlay position, and server URLs) is stored in the app’s private storage. Android backup and transfer are governed by your device settings and the installed version’s backup rules.

3. Network connections LiveCaptionN may make

Vosk speech recognition and ML Kit translation can run on-device once models are installed. In editions that offer remote processing, the selected backends and device capabilities determine whether captioning and translation can run locally. Network use also includes the services below and the advertising, diagnostics, billing, and feedback services described in this policy.

  • Vosk speech model downloads. When you tap Manage on-device models and pick a new language, the app downloads the model zip from alphacephei.com, unzips it to app-private storage, and then never contacts that server again for that model.
  • Google ML Kit translation model downloads. The first time you use a given language pair, Google ML Kit downloads a ~30 MB translation model from Google’s model service. After that the translation step is fully offline. The download is performed by Google Play Services on your behalf — see Google’s ML Kit terms and Google’s privacy policy for details about that traffic.
  • Update checks. Editions that check for GitHub releases send the usual network metadata, such as the IP address and HTTP client information, when requesting release information.
  • LibreTranslate. When this backend is active, recognized text is sent to the configured LibreTranslate service. That service’s operator handles the text under its own practices.
  • Remote Whisper. When remote Whisper is active, captured audio is sent to the configured Whisper service. Some older editions can select remote modes when the device cannot run on-device models. Before captioning sensitive material, check the active backend and the chosen service’s privacy practices.

On a compatible device, downloaded Vosk and ML Kit models allow local captioning and translation without a network connection when both local engines are selected. Ads, model downloads, remote modes and submitting feedback still require a connection.

4. Advertising

LiveCaptionN can display ads served by Google AdMob within the app’s own interface, not in the floating caption overlay.

AdMob can collect and share data such as device or other identifiers, IP address (which can be used to estimate approximate location), app and ad interactions, and diagnostics to deliver and measure ads, provide analytics, and prevent fraud. See Google’s privacy policy and Google’s advertising policy. LiveCaptionN does not send captured caption audio or saved transcripts to AdMob as advertising inputs.

Use the ad choices shown by your version of LiveCaptionN, where available, and Google’s ad settings to manage ad personalization. These settings do not remove ads or control Firebase diagnostics.

5. Analytics and crash reporting

Release builds use Firebase Crashlytics, Firebase Analytics, and Firebase Performance Monitoring. Google processes usage events and technical information such as app or device identifiers, app and OS versions, crash logs, and performance diagnostics for app analytics and reliability. Analytics can record supported Play purchase events; it does not receive your card number from LiveCaptionN. See Google’s privacy policy for how Google handles this data.

6. Subscriptions and billing

LiveCaptionN is free to install and has free captioning features. Ad-Free and Pro are optional monthly upgrades. Some features require Pro. LiveCaptionN does not require an app account, but Google Play subscriptions are linked to the Play account used to buy them.

  • Play Store build. Google Play handles payment and renewal; LiveCaptionN uses Play purchase records to activate and restore subscriptions but does not receive your card details.
  • GitHub-distributed build. Purchases are handled by Stripe via a small backend the app talks to. The app sends only the email address you enter and, after a successful purchase, a Stripe-issued session identifier used to verify that purchase — it never sends card details, which are entered directly into Stripe’s own hosted checkout page. Managing or canceling a subscription happens on Stripe’s hosted Customer Portal, which verifies your identity by email itself.

App review demo

The review edition has an optional sign-in for authorized app reviewers. When a reviewer signs in, the app sends the assigned demo account name, password and a one-time request challenge over HTTPS to an isolated service hosted on Cloudflare. The service uses the connection IP address to limit repeated attempts, and issues a temporary review grant to the app when the credentials match. It does not store submitted passwords or caption content. The hosting provider may process network and operational metadata under its own privacy terms. This demo does not access customer purchases, restore subscriptions, or transmit captured audio, transcripts or saved history. Ordinary customers do not need this sign-in.

7. Public feedback and screenshots

When you submit an in-app report or a reply, text you send can appear on the project’s public GitHub issue tracker. A name or email address you enter can appear in the public report. An attached screenshot can be uploaded before the report is created, so do not attach sensitive material. Review what you send. If the diagnostics option is checked, the report can include technical information such as app and Android versions, device and language settings, memory and storage information, and the report time. Some versions start with that option checked; you can turn it off before sending.

8. What LiveCaptionN does not do

  • Ordinary users do not need a LiveCaptionN account; the separate demo sign-in is for app review only.
  • LiveCaptionN does not receive your card details. The service handling a Play or Stripe transaction depends on the edition you installed.
  • No background audio capture — audio is only read while a captioning session is actively running and a foreground service notification is visible.
  • LiveCaptionN does not read your contacts, calendar, SMS or call logs to provide captions. You choose any file or screenshot you attach and any location where you export captions.
  • LiveCaptionN does not require an app login to start captions.

9. Permissions the app requests and why

  • RECORD_AUDIO — needed to capture microphone audio for speech recognition. Only used while a captioning session is active.
  • SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW — needed to draw the floating caption window on top of other apps. No data crosses this boundary; it is a rendering permission.
  • FOREGROUND_SERVICE, FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MICROPHONE, FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PROJECTION — required by Android 14+ so the captioning service can run with a persistent notification while you switch to another app.
  • POST_NOTIFICATIONS — used for the captioning session notification and for optional in-app update alerts.
  • INTERNET — for network services described in this policy.

LiveCaptionN does not request GPS location for captioning. IP-based approximate location used by advertising is described above.

10. Children

LiveCaptionN is a general-purpose accessibility tool and is not directed at children under 13. It does not knowingly collect any information from children.

12. Contact

For non-sensitive issues, visit the project’s public support tracker. Do not include private information in a public issue. For a privacy question, a request about your data, or a request to remove a public report or image, use the developer’s private contact form. In the subject, write “LiveCaptionN privacy”. For a public report, include its link, but don’t send a new copy of sensitive data. Removing a report or attachment from the project cannot erase copies someone else already made.
